title: Limit check rule
rationale: The flight manuals of comparable aircraft treat the limit lines as permitted. A single rule for every type, at take-off and at landing, keeps the check consistent.
---

A loading is within limits if and only if its total mass is at most the maximum take-off mass and its centre of gravity is neither forward of the forward limit nor aft of the aft limit of the envelope. The rule is applied twice: at take-off with the usable fuel at take-off, and at landing with the usable fuel at take-off minus the planned fuel burn. Each limit is evaluated on its own:

- The total mass is compared with the maximum take-off mass.
- The centre of gravity is compared with the forward and the aft limit of the envelope at the total mass; when the total mass is above the maximum take-off mass, at the maximum take-off mass; when it is below the lowest mass of the envelope, at that lowest mass.
- When both a mass limit and a centre of gravity limit are exceeded, both limit indications are shown, the mass first.

The boundary of the envelope and the maximum take-off mass itself count as within limits. A loading in which any entry is missing (left empty) or not a valid entry gets the limit indication "Check not complete" at take-off and at landing, and never "Within limits".
